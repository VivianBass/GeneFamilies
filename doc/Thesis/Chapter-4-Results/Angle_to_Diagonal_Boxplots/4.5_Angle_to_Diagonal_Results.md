### **Overview: Tissue Versatility**

**Tissue Versatility** measures how consistently a gene is expressed across tissues. It is quantified as the cosine of the angle (\(\theta\)) between a gene's expression vector \(\mathbf{x}\) and a diagonal vector \(\mathbf{d}\), representing uniform expression.  

- **Small Angle**: Indicates uniform expression, signifying higher tissue versatility.  
- **Large Angle**: Reflects tissue-specific expression, where the gene is active in some tissues and less so in others.  

### **Purpose**

1. **Quantifying Versatility**: The angle to the diagonal provides a measure of how evenly a gene's expression is distributed across tissues.  

2. **Comparative Analysis**: This metric enables the comparison of gene expression patterns, helping to identify genes with broad or tissue-specific expression profiles and their potential biological roles.  


The boxplots display the distribution of angle values for each gene group, with mean values labeled within each box. Statistical significance from t-tests and Wilcoxon tests is shown, along with gene group counts for context.  

- **Regular Expression**: Based on raw expression values.  
- **Log2 Expression**: Based on log2-transformed expression values.
