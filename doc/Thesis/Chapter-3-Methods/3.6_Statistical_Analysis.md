
## **Statistical Analysis of Expression Distances**


### **rstatix Package**

**Purpose**:  

The rstatix package facilitates statistical tests, such as t-tests and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, to identify significant differences in expression profile distances (e.g., Euclidean distances) between gene groups (e.g., orthologs vs. paralogs). These tests assess metrics like mean/median distances, expression angles, and tissue specificity, ensuring robust conclusions about gene activity patterns in expression data. T-tests (parametric) and Wilcoxon tests (non-parametric) validate whether observed differences are statistically significant.


## **Statistical Analysis Methods**

### 1. _T-Test Analysis_

$$
\begin{align*}
H_0&: \mu_1 \leq \mu_2 \\
H_1&: \mu_1 > \mu_2 \\
\\
t &= \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}} \\
\\
\end{align*}
$$

### T-Test: *Overview and Application*  

A t-test is a parametric statistical method used to compare the means of two groups, determining whether their difference is statistically significant. This test assumes that the data follows a normal distribution and that variances are relatively similar between groups.  

In the context of gene expression analysis, t-tests are instrumental in identifying differences in key metrics, such as mean or median expression distances, tissue-specific distances, or expression angles, across gene groups like orthologs and paralogs. By evaluating these differences, researchers can uncover meaningful patterns in gene activity and relationships between gene groups.  

The hypotheses for a t-test are:  
- **Null Hypothesis (\( H_0 \))**: The means of the two groups are equal, indicating no significant difference.  
- **Alternative Hypothesis (\( H_1 \))**: The means of the two groups are not equal, or one is greater than the other (in the case of a one-sided test).  

By applying pairwise t-tests, researchers assess whether observed differences in gene expression metrics are statistically significant, providing insights into gene functionality and tissue-specific activity.

### 2. _Wilcoxon Rank-Sum Test_

$$
\begin{align*}
H_0&: P(X > Y) \leq 0.5 \\
H_1&: P(X > Y) > 0.5 \\
\\
W &= \sum_{i=1}^{n_1} R_i - \frac{n_1(n_1 + 1)}{2} \\
\\
z &= \frac{W - \mu_W}{\sigma_W} \\
\text{where } \mu_W &= \frac{n_1n_2}{2} \\
\sigma_W &= \sqrt{\frac{n_1n_2(n_1 + n_2 + 1)}{12}}
\end{align*}
$$

The Wilcoxon Rank-Sum Test, also known as the Mann-Whitney U Test, is a non-parametric statistical method used to compare two groups. It evaluates whether the values in one group tend to be stochastically greater than those in the other by analyzing the ranks of the data rather than the raw values.  

This test is especially useful when the assumptions of a t-test—such as normality or homogeneity of variances—are not met, making it a robust alternative for analyzing skewed or non-normal data.  

In the context of gene expression analysis, the Wilcoxon test is applied to metrics such as mean/median distances or expression angles to determine whether one group (e.g., orthologs) consistently shows higher values than another group (e.g., paralogs).  

The hypotheses for a one-sided Wilcoxon test are:  
- **Null Hypothesis (\( H_0 \))**: The values in group1 are not stochastically greater than those in group2.  
- **Alternative Hypothesis (\( H_1 \))**: The values in group1 are stochastically greater than those in group2.  

By leveraging the Wilcoxon test, researchers can assess significant differences in ranked data, providing insights into gene expression patterns even in the absence of normal distribution or equal variance assumptions.

### **Parameters Used for Statistical Tests**  


#### **Test Direction and Group Comparisons**

- **Two-Sided Test**: Tests for any difference between groups, regardless of direction, and is unbiased to group order.  
- **One-Sided Test**: Tests if one group has values greater ("greater") or less ("less") than the other, with the directionality affecting interpretation.  
- **Bidirectional Testing**: Both directions (group1 vs. group2 and group2 vs. group1) are tested to capture all possible significant differences between groups.


#### **3. Testing Correction**  

- **Method**: _Benjamini-Hochberg_ (BH) adjustment to control the False Discovery Rate (FDR).  
- **Purpose**: Reduces the likelihood of false positives when performing multiple comparisons.  

#### **4. Significance Levels** 

- p_adj < 0.001: *** (Highly significant)  
- p_adj < 0.01: ** (Significant)  
- p_adj < 0.05: * (Marginally significant)  
- p_adj ≥ 0.05: ns (Not significant)

#### **5. Statistical Outputs**  

- For both tests (T-Test and Wilcoxon):  

  - **Test Type**: Specifies if it’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum.  
  - **P-Values**: Includes raw \( p \) and adjusted \( p_{\text{adj}} \) values.  
  - **Statistic**: T-statistic (t-test) or Wilcoxon statistic (W).  
  - **Significance Annotation**: Indicates significance based on adjusted \( p \)-values.  


- example Code how the t-tests and wilcoxon tests were calculated


```R
  perform_tests <- function(data, valid_groups, analysis_type) {
      if (length(valid_groups[[analysis_type]]) >= 2) {

          t_test_result <- data %>%
              filter(Type %in% valid_groups[[analysis_type]]) %>%
              t_test(Distance ~ Type, alternative = "greater") %>%
              adjust_pvalue(method = "BH") %>%
              mutate(analysis = analysis_type, 
                    test_type = "t-test",
                    p.adj.signif = case_when(
                        p.adj >= 0.05 ~ "ns",
                        p.adj < 0.001 ~ "***",
                        p.adj < 0.01 ~ "**",
                        p.adj < 0.05 ~ "*"
                    ))
          
          wilcox_result <- data %>%
              filter(Type %in% valid_groups[[analysis_type]]) %>%
              wilcox_test(Distance ~ Type, alternative = "greater") %>%
              adjust_pvalue(method = "BH") %>%
              mutate(analysis = analysis_type, 
                    test_type = "wilcox",
                    p.adj.signif = case_when(
                        p.adj >= 0.05 ~ "ns",
                        p.adj < 0.001 ~ "***",
                        p.adj < 0.01 ~ "**",
                        p.adj < 0.05 ~ "*"
                    ))
          
          return(list(t_test = t_test_result, wilcox = wilcox_result))
      }
      return(NULL)
  }
```
