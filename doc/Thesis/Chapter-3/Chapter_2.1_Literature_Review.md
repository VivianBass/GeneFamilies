- _"Interspecies Comparative Analyses Reveal Distinct Carbohydrate-Responsive Systems among Drosophila Species."_

- from: https://www.cell.com/cell-reports


- **Abstract**

The study investigates how different Drosophila species respond to carbohydrate-rich diets, revealing distinct variations in gene regulation and metabolic pathways.



### Species Classification and Dietary Patterns

**Generalist Species:**
- D. melanogaster and D. simulans are generalist species that thrive on diverse food sources
- Their diet includes various rotting fruits, vegetables, and plant matter
- These food sources naturally contain varying levels of carbohydrates

**Specialist Species:**
- D. sechellia is a specialist species that feeds exclusively on specific plants
- Its primary food source is Morinda citrifolia
- This specialized diet requires unique metabolic adaptations



### Experimental Diet Types

The study analyzed three distinct diet types:

1. **M Diet (Carbohydrate-rich)**
   - High in carbohydrates
   - Low in proteins

2. **P Diet (Protein-rich)**
   - High in proteins
   - Low in carbohydrates

3. **C Diet (Control)**
   - Balanced nutrient composition
   - Moderate levels of both carbohydrates and proteins
   - Serves as a reference point for the study



### Key Findings

The research revealed significant differences between generalist and specialist species:

**Generalist Species (D. melanogaster and D. simulans):**

- Possess advanced carbohydrate-responsive gene regulation
- Utilize the TGF-β/Activin signaling pathway
- Successfully maintain metabolic balance across varied diets
- Demonstrate high dietary adaptability

**Specialist Species (D. sechellia):**

- Lacks robust carbohydrate-responsive regulation
- Shows poor adaptation to carbohydrate-rich diets
- Exhibits metabolite accumulation
- Demonstrates limited dietary flexibility
