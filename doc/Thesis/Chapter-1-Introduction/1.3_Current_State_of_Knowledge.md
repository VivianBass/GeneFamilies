## _A. Current State of Knowledge_

> regarding the Research Question and Hypothesis

* **Evolutionary Relationships**

  - Definition:
    - Orthology and paralogy are relationships between homologous genes
    - Divergence occurs through speciation or duplication events
    - First described by Fitch (1970)
    - Fundamental to understanding gene evolution

* **Expression Patterns**

  - Orthologs:
    - Show higher expression similarity across species
    - Maintain consistent patterns despite speciation
    - Express in similar tissues/conditions
    - Demonstrate functional conservation
  
  - Paralogs:
    - Display more diverse expression patterns
    - Show tissue-specific variations
    - Exhibit greater expression divergence
    - Reflect functional specialization

* **Functional Conservation**

  - Orthologs:
    - Tend to maintain ancestral functions
    - Conserve biological roles across species
    - Show functional stability over time
    - Support evolutionary conservation
  
  - Paralogs:
    - Frequently undergo functional divergence
    - Develop new or specialized functions
    - Show greater functional plasticity
    - Enable evolutionary innovation

* **Supporting Research**

  - Multiple studies confirm these patterns:
    - Altenhoff et al. (2012)
    - Gabaldon and Koonin (2013)
    - Rogozin et al. (2014)
    - Consistent findings across research groups

  - _Chen and Zhang (2012)_ findings:
    - Analyzed large RNAseq dataset from multiple tissues
    - Found higher expression similarity between orthologs
    - Supported the Ortholog Conjecture (OC)
    - Refuted the cellular context hypothesis

  - Key Conclusions:
    - Expression similarity higher in orthologs
    - Functional conservation supported
    - Multiple independent validations
    - Consistent across different approaches

* **Ortholog Conjecture (OC)**
    
    - Core Concept:
    - Orthologous genes maintain more similar functions across species
    - Functional similarity persists despite species divergence
    - Applies to genes with same level of sequence divergence
    - Fundamental principle in evolutionary genomics

    - States that orthologous genes are functionally more similar than paralogs
    - Supported by expression profile analyses
    - Backed by multiple studies including _Huerta-Cepas et al. (2011)_

    - The results from _Chen and Zhang (2012)_ analyzing a large RNAseq dataset from multiple tissues align with the OC

    Ortholog Conjecture (OC): The OC posits that orthologous genes, which are genes in different species that evolved from a common ancestral gene, are functionally more similar than paralogous genes at the same level of sequence divergence. The results from Chen and Zhang (2012) analyzing a large RNAseq dataset from multiple tissues align with the OC, as they found that the similarity of expression profiles between orthologs is significantly higher than that between within-species paralogs. This supports the notion that orthologs exhibit greater functional and expression similarities compared to paralogs.

    Refuting the Cellular Context Hypothesis: The findings also refute the cellular context hypothesis, which suggests that functional similarity of homologous genes is primarily determined by the cellular context of the genes, rather than evolutionary history. The results from the analysis of expression profiles indicate that the differences in expression patterns between orthologs and paralogs are consistent with evolutionary divergence rather than being primarily influenced by the cellular context.


* **Cellular Context Hypothesis**

    - The cellular context hypothesis suggests that the functional similarity of genes is primarily determined by the cellular environment in which the genes operate, rather than their evolutionary relationships.
    - Challenged by recent research findings
    - Less supported than the Ortholog Conjecture
