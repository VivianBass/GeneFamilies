- _"Interspecies Comparative Analyses Reveal Distinct Carbohydrate-Responsive Systems among Drosophila Species."_

- from: https://www.cell.com/cell-reports

- **Abstract**

   This study investigates the influence of dietary patterns on carbohydrate-responsive systems across different *Drosophila* species, focusing on the metabolic and genetic distinctions between generalist and specialist species. Using the fruit fly family (*Drosophilidae*)—a valuable model organism with a well-annotated genome of 13,600 protein-coding genes and conserved genetic pathways shared with humans—the research examines how *D. melanogaster*, *D. simulans*, and *D. sechellia* adapt to carbohydrate-rich, protein-rich, and balanced diets. Generalist species (*D. melanogaster* and *D. simulans*) demonstrate advanced carbohydrate-responsive gene regulation, leveraging the TGF-β/Activin signaling pathway to maintain metabolic balance and high dietary adaptability. In contrast, the specialist species *D. sechellia*, adapted to the specific diet of *Morinda citrifolia*, exhibits limited carbohydrate regulation, metabolite accumulation, and poor adaptation to carbohydrate-rich diets. These findings highlight evolutionary differences in metabolic pathways and gene regulation, emphasizing the significance of *Drosophila* as a model for studying dietary specialization, adaptability, and broader genetic and evolutionary mechanisms.
